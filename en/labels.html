Lineage of Labels: Evolution of Psychiatric Concepts

Decoupled from synthetic curves, this timeline plots documented milestones in the invention and dismantling of gendered diagnostic categories.

c. 5th–4th Century BC

Hippocratic Corpus: The Wandering Womb (Hystera)

Origins of 'hysteria'; anatomical myth positing that the uterus moved through the body causing suffocation unless restrained by marriage and childbearing.

Historical Citation: Hippocratic Corpus, Diseases of Women
Contemporary figures in archive: Cleopatra VII
2nd Century AD

Galenic Medicine: Humoral Theory & Melancholia

Systematization of black bile (melancholia) and gendered humoral temperaments, framing female psychology as innately passive and unstable.

Historical Citation: Galen, On the Affected Parts
Contemporary figures in archive: Hypatia of Alexandria
1486 AD

Malleus Maleficarum (The Hammer of Witches)

Inquisitorial manual codified the demonization of female intellect and defiance as witchcraft and satanic treason.

Historical Citation: Institoris & Sprenger, Malleus Maleficarum (1486)
Contemporary figures in archive: Isabella I of Castile, Catherine de' Medici
1790s AD

Philippe Pinel & Institutional Moral Treatment

Unchaining of psychiatric patients at the Salpêtrière; institutional birth of modern psychiatry as a specialized medical discipline.

Historical Citation: Pinel, Traité médico-philosophique sur l'aliénation mentale (1801)
Contemporary figures in archive: Mary Shelley
1835 AD

James Cowles Prichard: 'Moral Insanity'

Introduction of 'moral insanity' to categorize emotional defiance without intellectual impairment, heavily applied to non-conforming Victorian women.

Historical Citation: Prichard, A Treatise on Insanity and Other Disorders Affecting the Mind (1835)
Contemporary figures in archive: Queen Victoria, Mary Shelley
1870s–1880s AD

Jean-Martin Charcot: Hysteria as Staged Spectacle

Public Tuesday lectures and medical photography turning female hysteria into a codified performative spectacle.

Historical Citation: Charcot, Leçons sur les maladies du système nerveux (1885)
Contemporary figures in archive: Queen Victoria
1895 AD

Freud & Breuer: Studies on Hysteria

Foundational text of psychoanalysis; reinterpreted hysterical somatic symptoms as symbolic conversions of repressed psychological trauma.

Historical Citation: Freud & Breuer, Studien über Hysterie (1895)
Contemporary figures in archive: Queen Victoria, Simone de Beauvoir
1952 AD

Publication of the First DSM (DSM-I)

First official American psychiatric nomenclature standardizing diagnostic criteria following WWII military psychiatric observations.

Historical Citation: American Psychiatric Association, Diagnostic and Statistical Manual: Mental Disorders (1952)
Contemporary figures in archive: Simone de Beauvoir
1980 AD

DSM-III: Removal of Hysteria, Creation of Histrionic & BPD

Historic paradigm shift removing the classical term 'hysteria', introducing categorical criteria for Histrionic and Borderline Personality Disorders.

Historical Citation: APA, DSM-III (1980)
Contemporary figures in archive: Princess Diana
2013 AD

DSM-5 Alternative Model for Personality Disorders (AMPD)

Transitional framework in Section III replacing all-or-nothing categorical labels with continuous dimensional trait domains.

Historical Citation: APA, DSM-5 Section III (2013)
2022 AD

WHO ICD-11: Complete Dimensional Overhaul

WHO's official diagnostic manual completely abandons historic categories in favor of dimensional severity and trait domain specifiers.

Historical Citation: World Health Organization, ICD-11 (2022)
