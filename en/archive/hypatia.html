Late Antique Alexandria · c. 370 AD – 415 AD

Hypatia of Alexandria

Ὑπατία

Mathematician, Neoplatonist philosopher, and political advisor to Alexandria's civic leadership; an enduring figure of intellectual autonomy in Late Antiquity.

«Reserve your right to think, for even to think wrongly is better than not to think at all.»
Elbert Hubbard, Little Journeys to the Homes of Great Teachers (1908) Apocryphal

Apocryphal quote originating in 1908 modern literature, demonstrating posthumous romantic myth-making.

۰۱

What She Did (The Record)

c. 400 AD Becomes head of the Neoplatonist school in Alexandria, teaching mathematics, geometry, and astronomy.
412 AD Acts as chief civic counselor to Roman prefect Orestes during escalating political tensions.
415 AD Targeted political assassination by religious zealots following accusations of witchcraft.
«Witch & Demonic Manipulator»
John of Nikiu (7th-century chronicler) · 7th Century

Use of astronomical instruments, mathematical science, and political influence over Orestes.

Personality Reading (Non-Diagnostic)

Methodological Note: This is not a clinical diagnosis. Retrospective diagnosis without clinical interview is epistemologically impossible. What follows is a non-pathologizing synthesis of documented history.

Hypatia's murder reflected acute municipal power struggles in Alexandria. Contemporary sources praised her civic leadership; subsequent hostile chroniclers invented tales of sorcery to exonerate civic violence.
